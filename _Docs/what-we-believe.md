# What We Believe
### Alexandra's — the values, and how we use them

> Internal working document. This is the spine: what we grow in a child, what
> we hire for, and what we want the right families to recognise in us. It is not
> parent-facing copy as written — it's the tool the copy, the hiring, and the
> tour all come *from*. Keep it where the team can see it; lift from it, don't
> bury it.

---

## What every child takes with them

Three things — in this order, because the order is the point.

**Belonging.** A child who is known and held, exactly as they are, is the only
child who will risk being seen. Belonging isn't the reward at the end; it's the
ground everything else stands on — and it includes the parts of a child that
come from somewhere else. We add to who a child is. We never ask them to
subtract.

**Courage.** Once a child feels safe to be seen, they'll try — and be wrong out
loud, and try again. This is the one we were built around: the bravery to take
the stage, in every sense of it.

**Curiosity.** A child brave enough to be wrong keeps wondering. This is the
learning that outlasts any single fact — knowing how to ask, guess, test, and
ask again.

If we only get one of these into a child, make it **belonging** — the other two
grow from it. If we get all three, we've done the job.

## What we believe — and what it commits us to

A value only counts if it makes us *do* something and *refuse* something. Here's
the belief, and the rule it creates.

**The child is capable.** → We follow the child and resist the urge to
over-help. We'd rather a child work through a hard thing than have an adult
quietly solve it for them.

**Play is real learning.** → We don't apologise for play or call it "just"
anything. We'd refuse to turn the day into worksheets to look more "serious" to
a worried parent.

**Structure serves the child — never the reverse.** → We use structure where it
builds focus and security, and drop it where it only serves the adults' need for
order. A routine that exists purely for tidiness doesn't survive here.

**Nature is a classroom.** → Outdoor time, weather, mud, and managed risk are
core learning, not recess. The yard is not where children burn off energy
between the "real" lessons — it *is* a real lesson.

**Belonging without erasure.** → We keep a child's home language and heritage in
the room, not at the door. We will not make a child smaller to help them fit in.

**Ready for life, not just for school.** → We judge ourselves on resilience,
curiosity, and how a child treats others — not only on letters and numbers. We
won't trade a child's confidence for an earlier worksheet.

**Effort builds ability.** → We praise the trying, not the talent. "He's just
not a maths person" is not a thing anyone says about a four-year-old here.

## What we don't say

The test for any value: *would a respectable competitor refuse to sign it?* If
not, it's wallpaper — cut it.

These all fail the test, so we don't use them: *nurturing, fun, holistic, safe,
passionate, child-centred, thriving, second home, fostering creativity.* Every
centre claims them, which means they filter no one and tell a parent nothing.
Warmth comes from the specific scene — a tower that fell three times, a worm
after rain — never the adjective.

## How we use this page

**Hiring.** Read the belief statements aloud to a candidate and watch for the
nod or the flinch. "The child is capable — so we let them struggle" sorts
educators fast: the ones who lean in are ours; the ones who reach to rescue,
however kindly, are not. Skill we can train. These convictions we can't.

**The tour.** Each value should be *visible*, not just spoken — pair every claim
with something a parent can see:
- *Belonging* → home languages on the wall; a child's culture present in the room.
- *The child is capable* → an educator hanging back while a child works a problem out.
- *Nature is a classroom* → the outdoor space mid-use, not tidy and empty.
- *Play is real learning* → the documentation and thinking wall that shows what the play taught.

A parent who *sees* a value lived will believe it. One who only hears it won't.

**Families.** We don't need every parent — we need the right ones. Said plainly
and lived visibly, these beliefs let families who share them recognise us, and
let the ones hunting for a worksheet factory self-select out before the tour.
That isn't lost business. It's a better-matched room.
